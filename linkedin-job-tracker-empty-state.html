Portfolio/Localization QA

Case study · UX writing & Italian localization QA

Helping people find the right next step.

A proposed Italian empty state for a job-tracking experience—designed to distinguish saved opportunities from submitted applications and make the next action clear.

Concept statusIndependent · not commissioned · not implemented. The possible user confusion described here is a heuristic hypothesis, not a research finding.
Contribution

Heuristic review, message hierarchy, and Italian microcopy proposal.

Scope

LinkedIn Job Tracker · Italian · mobile and web.

Focus

State clarity, action clarity, and separation of user journeys.

The challenge

An empty screen should still tell people where to go.

In the flow reviewed, the tracker opens on the “Salvata” (“Saved”) tab. Someone looking for submitted applications may encounter an empty screen and be unsure where to find them. The original text also repeats information without clearly guiding the next action.

This is a possible usability issue identified in a heuristic review. It would need validation with people using the experience; it is not presented as a confirmed finding.

Proposed Italian microcopy

One clear state, two distinct paths

The hierarchy first explains what is missing and how to find opportunities, then offers a separate route for submitted applications.

Empty-state title

Nessuna offerta salvata

“No saved jobs” — names the specific content missing from this view.

Supporting text

Salva le opportunità che ti interessano per ritrovarle qui.

“Save opportunities that interest you to find them here.” — connects the action to its benefit.

Primary action

Esplora le offerte

“Explore jobs” — makes the next step explicit and predictable.

Alternate path

Candidature inoltrate

“Submitted applications” — provides a distinct route for people looking for applications already sent.

Localization & content decisions

Small wording choices, clearer navigation

01Specify the state. “Nessuna offerta salvata” identifies what is absent instead of relying on a generic empty message.
02Explain the benefit. The supporting line tells people why saving an opportunity is useful: they can find it again here.
03Make the CTA predictable. “Esplora le offerte” communicates the action, rather than repeating the empty-state information.
04Separate the journeys. Saved opportunities and submitted applications are presented as different destinations.

Validation plan

Test the hypothesis before claiming impact.

This is a concept proposal, not a released change. No usability data or product metrics are available, so no measured outcome is claimed.

In a usability test, I would check whether people can distinguish the two sections, reach the right one without hesitation, and predict where the primary CTA leads. I would also compare interaction with the CTA against the original version.
