Portfolio/Speech-quality evaluation

Case study · Italian audio QA

Giving speech-quality issues a shared language.

An anonymized bilingual taxonomy for turning subtle observations about spoken or synthetic Italian into precise, reusable evaluation labels.

Project noteThis is an original portfolio reconstruction inspired by professional experience. It does not reproduce confidential project materials, audio, prompts, internal guidelines, or platform screenshots.
Challenge

Describe similar auditory issues consistently without collapsing distinct causes into one label.

Approach

Separate five defect families and align Italian descriptions with concise English labels.

Value

Make feedback clearer, more comparable, and more actionable across multilingual teams.

The challenge

Unnatural speech can have very different causes.

A recording may sound unnatural because of pronunciation, voice quality, prosody, paralinguistic behavior, or a technical artefact. Without a shared taxonomy, evaluators may describe similar observations inconsistently, making comparison and quality reporting harder.

The challenge was to distinguish these layers while keeping terminology clear in both Italian and English.

Framework

Five complementary categories

For each observation, the framework aligns an Italian description, a concise English label, a category, and wording that can be reused consistently in evaluation notes.

01

Voice quality & timbre

Resonance, phonation, and overall voice texture.

02

Pronunciation

Sound-level or syllable-level realization.

03

Prosody

Rhythm, timing, stress, and intonation.

04

Paralinguistic features

Breaths and other non-lexical vocal behavior.

05

Technical artefacts

Recording or generation issues distinct from vocal defects.

Structured diagnosis

Why a precise category matters

The right label points reviewers toward the relevant quality question and supports more targeted analysis.

Initial observation

“The voice sounds unusual at the beginning of the utterance.”

More precise diagnosis

“A hoarse or dysphonic voice onset affects voice quality at the start of phonation—not the identity of a specific phoneme.”

Value to the workflow

Clarity that makes review more useful.

✓Use more consistent defect labels.
✓Separate linguistic issues from technical artefacts.
✓Communicate clearly across Italian- and English-speaking teams.
✓Identify recurring patterns and make feedback actionable.

The framework does not replace expert judgment; it gives evaluators shared vocabulary for applying that judgment more consistently.
