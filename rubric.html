Detailed scoring framework

The Rubric.

A structured scoring rubric designed to assess the competencies of AI response evaluators. Each criterion defines the target behavior and describes four levels of evidence.

Criterion descriptors

On a phone, swipe each scoring table horizontally to read the full descriptor.

1. Bias Identification and Management

Definition: The candidate names at least two biases, describes how each could have altered their judgment, and identifies at least one countermeasure adopted.

ScoreLabelDescription
4ExemplaryNames ≥2 biases, explains impact on judgment, and provides ≥1 countermeasure with a concrete example.
3AdvancedNames ≥2 biases and explains impact, but the countermeasure is inadequate or absent.
2BeginnerNames only 1 bias or does not explain its impact on the evaluation.
1InadequateDoes not name specific biases; response is vague or absent.

2. Guideline Adherence

Definition: The candidate cites the most relevant criterion for the response evaluated and justifies the score based on received instructions rather than personal opinion.

ScoreLabelDescription
4ExemplaryCites the most relevant criterion and justifies the score with precise reference to instructions and a concrete example.
3AdvancedCites the criterion and assigns the score, but the justification is generic or based on personal opinion.
2BeginnerCites the criterion but does not justify the score based on guidelines.
1InadequateDoes not cite criteria, evaluates by intuition, or response is absent.

3. Written Feedback Quality

Definition: The candidate uses clear language, references specific parts of the evaluated text with timestamps, and indicates the issue found and a possible solution for each.

ScoreLabelDescription
4ExemplaryUses clear language, cites text with timestamps, identifies the issue, and proposes a solution for each.
3AdvancedUses clear language, cites text with timestamps, and identifies the issue, but does not provide an adequate solution.
2BeginnerUses clear language and cites text with timestamps, but does not indicate the issue or a solution.
1InadequateFeedback is vague, has no references to the text, or is absent.

4. Score Calibration

Definition: The candidate provides consistent scores for similar responses and, when scores differ, justifies the difference with specific references to the text.

ScoreLabelDescription
4ExemplaryProvides consistent scores for similar responses; differences are justified with specific text references.
3AdvancedProvides consistent scores for similar responses; differences are justified with vague text references.
2BeginnerProvides consistent scores for similar responses; differences are justified without text references.
1InadequateScores are inconsistent and have no justification.

5. Metacognition

Definition: The candidate confirms having followed the guidelines, acknowledges that personal experience may have influenced the evaluation, and identifies the specific bias that may have played a role.

ScoreLabelDescription
4ExemplaryConfirms guidelines were followed, acknowledges personal influence, and clearly identifies the specific bias.
3AdvancedConfirms guidelines were followed and acknowledges personal influence, but identifies the bias vaguely.
2BeginnerConfirms guidelines were followed and acknowledges personal influence, but does not identify what may have influenced it.
1InadequateShows no reflection on the evaluation process or self-awareness.

Scoring scale

Common score definitions

4

Exemplary

Expert behavior; complete and supported by concrete examples.

3

Advanced

Competent behavior; one key element is missing.

2

Beginner

Partial behavior; fundamental elements are missing.

1

Inadequate

Vague, absent, or off-topic response.
